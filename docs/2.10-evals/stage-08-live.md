4. **Wheels and tires:** Spin each wheel and watch for wobble (trueness). Squeeze spokes to check that they're tight and none are loose. Grab each wheel at the top and bottom and rock it to feel for bearing play. Check tire tread and sidewalls for cracks.

5. **Headset, bottom bracket and the test ride:** Lock the front brake, rock the bike forward and back to feel for headset looseness, and check the pedals and crank for play. On the test ride, listen for creaks, and make sure the bike tracks straight with no pull to one side. Also check that the seat post, handlebars and stem are tightly fastened.

## deep: Explain what an index fund is.
**user**: Explain what an index fund is.
**assistant**: An **index fund** is a fund that holds the same securities as a market index, in roughly the same proportions, so its return tracks that index rather than trying to beat it.

**How it works**

- An index is a list of securities chosen by rules, such as the S&P 500 (the 500 largest US companies) or the FTSE All-World (thousands of companies across developed and emerging markets).
- The fund buys all or a representative sample of those holdings. Most are weighted by market value, so bigger companies make up a bigger share.
- Nobody picks stocks. When the index changes, the fund adjusts its holdings to match. That's why it's called **passive** investing.
- You can usually buy one as a mutual fund or as an ETF (exchange-traded fund). ETFs trade like stocks during the day. Mutual funds price once a day.

**Why people use them**

- **Low fees.** Expense ratios are often between about 0.03% and 0.20% a year. Actively managed funds often charge 0.5% to 1% or more.
- **Instant diversification.** One fund can spread your money across hundreds or thousands of companies, which reduces the damage from any single company failing.
- **Beating most professionals.** S&P's SPIVA reports consistently show that most actively managed US large-cap funds underperform the S&P 500 over 10 to 15 years, mainly because of their higher fees. See [SPIVA U.S. Scorecard](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/research-insights/spiva/) for the latest figures.
- **Simplicity.** There's no need to research individual stocks or time the market.

**Edge cases and limits**

- **It still goes down.** An index fund falls when its market falls. In 2008 and 2020, broad index funds lost a third or more before recovering.
- **Concentration.** Market-cap weighting means a few giant companies can make up a large share of the index. Your fund may be more concentrated than the name suggests.
- **Tracking error.** The fund's return won't match the index exactly because of fees, cash, and timing of rebalancing. This is usually small for big funds.
- **Not all index funds are equal.** Niche, thematic or leveraged index funds can carry higher fees and far more risk.
- **Tax treatment varies.** In the US, ETFs are often more tax-efficient than mutual funds. Rules differ by country.
- **Availability differs.** Some countries restrict certain funds to residents, and minimum investments vary.

**Quick comparison**

| | Index fund | Actively managed fund |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Match the market | Beat the market |
| Typical fee | ~0.03%–0.20% | ~0.5%–1%+ |
| Turnover | Low | Often high |
| Manager decisions | Rules-based | Discretionary |

This is general information, not personal financial advice. If you're deciding where your own money goes, a licensed adviser can look at your timeline, taxes and risk tolerance.

## deep: How do I keep basil alive indoors?
**user**: How do I keep basil alive indoors?
**assistant**: Basil does best indoors with **bright light, warmth, and careful watering**. Most indoor basil failures come from too little light or too much water.

**Light**
- Give it **6+ hours of direct sun**, ideally a south- or west-facing window.
- If you don't have that, use a **grow light** for 12–14 hours a day. A window alone often makes basil leggy and weak.
- Turn the pot every few days so it grows evenly.

**Temperature and placement**
- Keep it between **18–24°C (65–75°F)**.
- Keep it away from cold drafts, radiators, and air conditioners. Basil is damaged by temperatures below about 10°C (50°F).

**Watering**
- Water when the **top 2.5 cm (1 inch) of soil is dry**. Check with your finger.
- Water at the base until it drains out the bottom, then empty the saucer. Soggy roots cause rot.
- Wilting in the afternoon can mean it needs water, but if the soil is already wet, the roots may be rotting.

**Pot and soil**
- Use a pot at least **15–20 cm (6–8 in) wide** with drainage holes.
- Use a light, well-draining potting mix. Garden soil compacts indoors.

**Feeding**
- Feed every **2–4 weeks** during active growth with a diluted liquid fertilizer (half strength) or a balanced organic feed.
- Don't feed a plant that's stressed or in low light. It can burn the roots.

**Harvesting (this keeps it alive longer)**
- Pinch off the top leaves just above a pair of leaves, and cut flower buds as soon as they appear. Flowering makes the leaves bitter and slows growth.
- Harvest regularly so it branches out instead of getting tall and thin.

**Common problems**

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow leaves, soggy soil | Overwatering | Let the soil dry out, check drainage |
| Crispy brown edges, droopy | Underwatering or dry air | Water more consistently, mist or group plants |
| Tall, thin, pale stems | Not enough light | Move to brighter light or add a grow light |
| Black, mushy stems at the base | Root rot | Repot in fresh dry soil, trim the rot |
| Tiny flies around the soil | Fungus gnats | Let the top soil dry fully between waterings |
| Sticky leaves, webbing, or clusters on stems | Aphids or spider mites | Rinse with water, then use insecticidal soap |

**Tip for store-bought basil:** Those pots usually hold 5–10 plants crowded together. Split them into separate pots as soon as you can, since crowding causes most of the early decline.

## deep: What should I check before signing a lease on a flat?
**user**: What should I check before signing a lease on a flat?
**assistant**: Before you sign, check these things. Local law varies, so I've kept this general and flagged where it matters.

## 1. The lease itself
- **Rent, deposit and fees:** the monthly rent, what the deposit covers, and whether it's refundable. Also check for admin, agency, or key fees.
- **Term and break clause:** how long the lease runs and whether you can leave early. Find out what early exit costs (often one or two months' rent) and whether you can sublet or assign.
- **Rent increases:** whether the lease allows mid-term increases. In many places it's limited or banned.
- **Repairs and maintenance:** who fixes what (boiler, appliances, damp, pests) and how fast the landlord must respond.
- **Notice period:** how much notice you must give to end the tenancy, and whether it matches your plans.
- **House rules:** pets, smoking, guests, subletting, decorating, and anything that would affect you.
- **Joint tenancy:** if others are on the lease, understand that each person can usually be liable for the full rent.

## 2. The property
- **Inventory (check-in report):** get a written list of the condition of everything, with photos, and sign it. This is what protects your deposit at move-out.
- **Damp, mould, and ventilation:** look in corners, behind furniture, and under sinks. Check for water stains on ceilings.
- **Water pressure, hot water, heating:** run the taps and check the boiler or heating system.
- **Windows, doors, locks:** check that they close, lock properly, and aren't drafty.
- **Noise:** visit at a busy time (evening or weekend) and listen for traffic, neighbours, and building noise.
- **Phone signal and internet speed:** test them yourself, not just on the listing.
- **Storage, parking, bins, and bike space:** confirm they're included and where they are.

## 3. Legal and admin
- **Landlord or agent identity:** confirm who actually owns the flat, and that the person you're dealing with has the right to rent it out.
- **Deposit protection:** in many countries the deposit must go into a government-backed scheme within a set time. Ask for the scheme name and reference.
- **Required certificates:** gas safety, electrical safety (EICR in the UK), energy performance certificate, and fire-safety documents, depending on where you are.
- **Building issues:** for flats, ask about service charges, ground rent, leasehold terms, and any planned major works.
- **Written everything:** any promise (repairs, decorating, a reduced rent) should be in the lease, not just said verbally.
- **Read it fully before signing:** if something is unclear, ask for it in writing. Consider having a local tenancy advice service or a solicitor read it if the lease is long or complicated.

## 4. Practical tips
- Compare the lease with the listing. If they differ, go with the lease and ask about the difference.
- Keep copies of everything: the signed lease, the inventory, receipts for the deposit, and messages with the landlord.
- Don't pay a deposit before you've seen the property and received the written tenancy terms.

## Edge cases to watch
- **Deposit paid before the lease is ready:** a common scam pattern. Be wary of anyone asking for money before a viewing or a contract.
- **Very cheap rent with cash-only requests:** an unusually low price plus pressure to pay quickly is a warning sign.
- **Short or unclear notice terms:** some leases auto-renew, so note the renewal date and what you need to do to stop it.

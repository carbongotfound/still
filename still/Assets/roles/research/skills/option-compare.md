## When
The user is choosing between two or more tools, vendors, products or plans.

## Inputs
- The options, or the need when the user wants you to find them.
- What matters most to them: price, features, limits, support. Recall it first, and assume the usual four when you don't know.

## Steps
1. Fix the list of options. When you find them yourself, keep it to the 3 to 5 that fit best and say why.
2. read_url each option's own pricing and feature pages.
3. Read one or two independent reviews for each with web_search and read_url.
4. Build one table: a row per option, a column per thing that matters, a link for every fact, prices as of today's date.
5. Pick the best fit for this user.

## Check
- Prices and limits come from the vendor's own page, with today's date.
- Each cell has a source, or says unknown.

## Deliverable
The pick in one line and why, the table, and what would change the pick. Save the table as a CSV with save_file when it has more than four rows.

## If it goes wrong
- A price is hidden behind "contact sales": write that, don't estimate.
- Two sources disagree on a feature: trust the vendor's current docs and note the conflict.

## Limits
Never rank on features you couldn't confirm, and never present a review's opinion as fact.
